DATA VISUALIZATION

Power BI + Tableau

My visualization projects range from interactive dashboards to multi-page data stories. I focus on how someone explores the information—not just the calculations behind each chart. Below, I distinguish finished dashboards and stories from earlier analytical worksheet collections.

TABLEAU · INTERACTIVE DASHBOARDU.S. Border & Port Crossings

Portfolio workbook: Assignment 4(1).twbx. An assembled Tableau dashboard for exploring border crossing volumes across ports, states, vehicle types, and time. It brings a geographic map, trends, rankings, and summary measures together in one analytical interface rather than leaving each view as a separate worksheet.

Interaction design: A parameter lets the viewer switch between port and state groupings, while dashboard interactions connect the different visual perspectives. The goal is to let an audience move from an overview to a specific location or category without needing to interpret the raw dataset.

TABLEAU · THREE-PART DATA STORYWeather, Congestion & Traffic Delays

Portfolio workbook: Assignment 5 Final(2).twbx. A three-stage Tableau Story examining Pennsylvania traffic congestion in relation to other Northeastern states. The analysis combines event maps, weather comparisons, visibility measures, traffic-delay charts, and geographic breakdowns to explore when and where transportation disruptions occur.

Story structure: (1) Pennsylvania in regional context, (2) congestion and delays by weather conditions, and (3) a closer look at rain and mist. The story moves from a broad comparison toward more specific questions and possible planning implications. Findings are descriptive; the views do not establish that weather or infrastructure alone caused observed differences.

POWER BI + TABLEAU · GROUP DATA STORYConnecticut Accidental Drug-Related Deaths

An iterative Power BI project and a three-part Tableau group story investigating accidental drug-related deaths in Connecticut from 2012 through 2023. The Tableau presentation progresses from overall trends to drug involvement and demographic patterns. It is an example of communicating sensitive public-health data through a structured sequence of views.

Presentation consideration: Drug involvement can overlap—for example, fentanyl is an opioid—so the charts should not be interpreted as mutually exclusive causes of death.

APPROACH · ANALYSIS TO INTERFACEWhat connects these projects

Building useful visualizations means deciding what the audience needs to see first, where filters or comparisons help, and when a sequence of views makes a complex question easier to understand. I used bar and line charts, maps, scatterplots, KPIs, dashboard actions, parameters, and data-story pages for different analytical purposes, while learning how to balance detail with clarity.
